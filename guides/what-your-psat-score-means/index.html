What your PSAT score means, and what to do next.

By Allan and Jon, the two coaches at Lighthouse Prep. Last reviewed October 6, 2026.

Send this page to a parent
Email the link

The short answer. A PSAT score is a starting point. The report shows your student's total (320–1520) and two section scores (160–760 each), how they compare with other students in the same grade, and which of eight skill areas cost the most points. For juniors, it is also the entry to National Merit. Read it to find where the points are, then pick a spring SAT® date and plan backwards from it.

When PSAT scores arrive

Students who took the PSAT/NMSQT® this October get their scores on one of three dates, depending on when their school submitted the tests:

Tests submitted byScores to students
Oct 9, 2026Oct 22, 2026
Oct 23, 2026Nov 5, 2026
Oct 30, 2026Nov 12, 2026

Students 13 and older who gave a mobile number on test day can see their scores in College Board's BigFuture School app. Schools also receive a PDF called “Your Score Report” for every student, and students 13 and older can log in to their own College Board account for more detail. If you can, get the full report: the skill breakdown is where the useful information is.

Two of these deserve a second look. The score range is a reminder that one sitting is a sample: plan from the range, not from a single number. And the percentile compares your student only with students in the same grade, so a sophomore's percentile is measured against sophomores.

What counts as a good PSAT score?

There is no single number. Websites and AI answers quote different “good” scores because they answer different questions. Four questions give you a useful answer instead:

  1. Is your student on track? Compare each section with its grade-level benchmark (for juniors, 460 in Reading and Writing and 510 in Math). At or above the benchmark means College Board considers your student on track to be college ready.
  2. How does your student compare with classmates nationally? The percentile answers that, grade by grade.
  3. What do the colleges on the list expect? See the next section.
  4. Is National Merit realistic? That depends on the Selection Index and your state's cutoff, covered below for juniors.

A score can be fine on one of these and short on another. A junior at the 80th percentile may still sit below the SAT range of the colleges they care about most, and that gap is where the plan starts.

Compare with the colleges on the list

The PSAT and SAT are on one common scale. College Board explains that a student with a 500 in PSAT Math would be expected to score about 500 in SAT Math if they took both on the same day; the SAT's scale runs higher, to 1600.

That makes a simple comparison possible:

  1. Find each college's middle 50% of SAT scores. Many colleges publish a Common Data Set, and section C9 lists the 25th and 75th percentile SAT scores of enrolled first-year students who submitted them.
  2. Put your student's PSAT total next to each range: below it, inside it, or above it.
  3. Look at the two sections. Check which one holds most of the gap: that is where to start.

Keep testing policies in mind as you do this. More selective colleges require the SAT or ACT again: MIT, Harvard, Yale, Stanford, Penn, Dartmouth, Brown, Cornell and Georgetown do for fall 2027, and all eight Ivy League colleges do for students applying for fall 2028, today's juniors (some offer waivers). Others, such as Duke, Northwestern and NYU, remain test-optional for fall 2027, and the University of California doesn't consider scores at all. Check each college's own testing page.

If your student is a junior

National Merit. Only the PSAT/NMSQT taken in the junior year counts for the National Merit Scholarship Program. About 1.3 million students enter each year, almost all of them juniors. Around 50,000 high scorers are recognized: about 34,000 become Commended Students on one national cutoff, and about 16,000 become Semifinalists. Semifinalist cutoffs are set state by state, so the Selection Index that qualifies in one state may not in another. Schools hear in April 2027 which students scored high enough for recognition, and students are notified through their schools in September 2027. Semifinalists who want to become Finalists need, among other things, an SAT or ACT score that confirms their PSAT result.

The spring SAT. For most juniors, the next step is an official SAT in the spring. The 2027 dates and the latest sensible start for a 12-week plan:

SAT dateRegister byStart preparing by (12 weeks before)
Mar 6, 2027Feb 19Dec 12, 2026

We start our own programs 12 weeks before a test because it leaves room for a full practice test, the fixes it points to, and a second practice test before the real one. Shorter plans can work when the gap is small.

If your student is a sophomore

A sophomore's PSAT is practice, and good practice. It doesn't count for National Merit, so the pressure is off, but the report is just as detailed. Compare each section with the 10th-grade benchmarks (430 in Reading and Writing, 480 in Math), look at the skill areas, and decide whether a calm plan this year would help before the junior-year PSAT/NMSQT next October. Schools that offer the PSAT 10 give it between March 1 and April 30, 2027, which gives a second reading before summer.

Read the Knowledge and Skills section first

This is the most useful part of the report. Each section has four content domains, and the report shows how your student did in each one:

Reading and WritingMath
Information and IdeasAlgebra
Craft and StructureAdvanced Math
Expression of IdeasProblem-Solving and Data Analysis
Standard English ConventionsGeometry and Trigonometry

The shortest bars show where the points are. Two students with the same total can need very different plans: one loses most of their points in a single Math domain, the other spreads small losses across all eight.

What to do next

  1. Get the full report, from the app, the school's PDF or your student's College Board account.
  2. Write down five numbers: both section scores, the total, the percentile and, for juniors, the Selection Index.
  3. Circle the two weakest skill areas.
  4. Pick a test date and count back 12 weeks.
  5. Start with free practice. Good free options: Official SAT Prep on Khan Academy®, full practice tests in College Board's Bluebook™ app, and free tutoring through Schoolhouse.world.
  6. Get a second opinion if you'd like one. We review a recent PSAT, SAT or practice test for free. Book a free 20-minute consult: a short call about your student's goals and timeline, a free review of the report, and a written plan within 24 hours of the review.

Frequently asked questions

Do colleges see PSAT scores?

College Board sends PSAT/NMSQT scores to your student's school and to the National Merit Scholarship Corporation. Colleges that require or consider tests ask for the SAT or ACT (a few also accept AP or IB), so the PSAT's jobs are practice, a skills report and, for juniors, National Merit entry. Colleges reach students through College Board's Connections program only if the student opts in.

Is a PSAT score the same as an SAT score?

They share one scale, so a PSAT score estimates where your student would score on the SAT that day. The PSAT tops out at 1520 and the SAT at 1600. Preparation helps most students, and how much depends on the student and where they start, so treat the PSAT as a baseline.

Does the 10th-grade PSAT count for National Merit?

No. Only the PSAT/NMSQT taken in the junior year (the third year of high school) counts. A sophomore who takes it now will need to take it again as a junior to enter the program.

How is the National Merit Selection Index calculated?

Double the Reading and Writing score, add the Math score, and divide by 10. A 620 in Reading and Writing and a 500 in Math give 174. The index runs from 48 to 228, and each state's Semifinalist cutoff is a Selection Index.

When will we know if my junior is a National Merit Semifinalist?

Schools are told in April 2027 which students scored high enough for recognition. Semifinalists and Commended Students are notified through their schools in September 2027, and Semifinalist names become public in mid-September.

My junior missed the PSAT. Is National Merit still possible?

Possibly. The National Merit Scholarship Corporation offers an alternate route of entry for students who miss the test for a qualifying reason. Your student must write to NMSC by April 1, 2027; the PSAT/NMSQT Student Guide explains what to include.

How long before the SAT should my student start preparing?

We plan 12 weeks before a test, which fits a full practice test, the fixes it points to and a second practice test. For the March 6 SAT, that means starting by December 12.
